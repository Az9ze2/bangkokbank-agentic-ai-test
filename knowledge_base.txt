Domestication and History
Cats were first domesticated around 10,000 years ago in the Near East, where wildcats began living alongside early farmers who stored grain that attracted rodents. Unlike dogs, cats largely domesticated themselves by choosing to live near humans for the reliable food source, rather than being selectively bred for specific tasks from the outset.

Domestication and History
Ancient Egyptians revered cats and associated them with the goddess Bastet, even mummifying them and imposing severe penalties on anyone who harmed one.

Behavior and Communication
Cats communicate through a combination of vocalizations, body language, and scent marking. A slow blink from a cat is often called a "cat kiss" and signals trust and contentment toward a person or another animal.

Behavior and Communication
Purring usually indicates relaxation, but cats also purr when stressed, injured, or giving birth, suggesting it may serve as a self-soothing mechanism. Cats are crepuscular, meaning they are most active at dawn and dusk, which relates to the natural hunting patterns of their wild ancestors.

Health, Lifespan, and Care
The average indoor cat lives between 13 and 17 years, with some cats reaching their early twenties with good care. Regular veterinary checkups, vaccinations, and dental care significantly extend a cat's healthy lifespan.

Health, Lifespan, and Care
Cats should have access to fresh water at all times, and many prefer running water from a fountain over a still bowl. Common health issues in older cats include kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and arthritis, so annual bloodwork is recommended for cats over seven years old.

Breeds
The Maine Coon is one of the largest domestic cat breeds, known for its tufted ears, bushy tail, and friendly, dog-like personality. Siamese cats are famous for their vocal nature, striking blue eyes, and short coat with distinctive color-point markings.

Breeds
The Sphynx is a hairless breed that requires regular bathing to remove oil buildup on its skin since it lacks a fur coat to absorb it. British Shorthairs are known for their dense, plush coats and calm, easygoing temperament, making them popular family pets.

Diet and Nutrition
Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning their bodies require nutrients found only in animal tissue, such as taurine, arachidonic acid, and vitamin A in its active form. A diet lacking sufficient taurine can lead to serious heart problems, including dilated cardiomyopathy, in cats.

Diet and Nutrition
Kittens generally need to eat more frequently than adult cats due to their higher energy requirements for growth, often three to four small meals a day. Common human foods that are toxic to cats include onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes, and raisins, and should never be offered as treats.
